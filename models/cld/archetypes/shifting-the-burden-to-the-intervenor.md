# Shifting the Burden to the Intervenor

## Signature

The same structure as Shifting the Burden, but the symptomatic fix is *another party* — a consultant, a vendor, a parent organization, an AI assistant. The intervenor solves the immediate problem. The host system never builds the muscle. The intervenor becomes load-bearing. Removing the intervenor is now risky; their fee or influence grows.

## Generic structure

Same as `shifting-the-burden.md` — only the interpretation of `symptomatic-fix` changes: it is an external actor's intervention rather than an internal workaround.

## Slots

| Slot | What goes here |
|---|---|
| `problem-symptom` | The recurring problem the host cannot or does not solve |
| `intervenor-action` | What the external party does to solve it |
| `host-capability` | The host's own ability to solve it — atrophies over time |
| `dependency` | The growing reliance on the intervenor |

## Recognition triggers

- "they handle it for us now"
- "we used to know how to do this"
- "vendor lock-in"
- "we can't run a project without them"

## Classic example

A company outsources its IT to a managed service provider. The MSP keeps things running. Over five years, internal IT capability shrinks to a procurement function. Switching providers becomes prohibitively expensive — the data, the runbooks, the relationships are all on the MSP's side.
